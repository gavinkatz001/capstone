### Common model input and evaluation

Each source adapter yields a source-prefixed participant ID, recording ID, placement, label, activity, and a `float32` 50 Hz matrix ordered `ax, ay, az, gx, gy, gz` in g and degrees/s. The notebook subtracts the per-window acceleration median to reduce differences between gravity-removed and gravity-included sources, then uses training-only mean and standard deviation. It extracts one strongest-motion window from each fall trial and spaced windows plus a strongest-motion window from ADL trials. This is weak event labeling; exact impact times are unavailable for several sources.

Splits are by participant **within each source**, before windowing. JasonKau participates in training only. The notebook reports overall and per-source held-out metrics and stores source/placement information in the checkpoint. Placement and capture protocol still differ: a shared six-column tensor does not guarantee a shared physical distribution. Evaluate wrist and waist slices separately before deploying any single model. The 50 Hz choice preserves the existing training and inference interface; UP-Fall's slower synchronized stream is interpolated and should be evaluated separately for any loss in sensitivity.

## Unified record contract

Each admitted source must produce a continuous recording with: `source`, source-prefixed `subject_id`, `recording_id`, `placement`, native sample rate, channel order, acceleration and gyroscope units, binary fall/ADL label, and optional fall type or event timestamps. The six numeric channels become `[ax, ay, az, gx, gy, gz]` as `float32`, acceleration in **g**, gyroscope in **degrees/second**, uniformly sampled at **50 Hz**. Verify axis mapping from each dataset's documentation. Convert m/s² by dividing by 9.80665, rad/s by multiplying by 180/π, downsample with antialiasing, and interpolate timestamped streams when needed. Reject nonfinite values, unknown units, missing axes, and recordings too short for a window. Preserve source/placement metadata for evaluation.

## First training protocol

1. Split by **participant within each source before windowing** so multiple trials or overlapping windows from one participant cannot cross train, validation, and test. Never treat Microchip or JasonKau trial numbers as proven participant IDs. Sources without participant IDs may contribute training data, but not the subject-independent metric.
2. Use **4-second windows at 50 Hz** (200 × 6). HR_IMU falls are only about 16–22 seconds; padding to 60 seconds would make most of every sample artificial. In a fall recording, take one window centered on the strongest acceleration magnitude as a provisional event window. Leave the rest unlabeled. From each non-fall recording, take non-overlapping windows plus one window around its strongest motion as a hard negative. Event timing remains uncertain because the dataset provides recording labels, not impact timestamps.
3. Subtract per-window acceleration median, then fit channel mean/std on training windows only. Use weighted sampling in training; choose a threshold on validation; score the held-out test subjects once. Report sensitivity, false-positive rate, AUC, confusion counts, split subject IDs, and recording-level metrics. These metrics describe simulated falls at mixed placements, not clinical performance or 60-second packet false-alarm rates.
4. Save weights with the channel order, sample rate, units, normalization, threshold, and split metadata. For deployment on 60-second packets, run a 4-second rolling scan and **recalibrate packet-level** thresholds and false-positive rates. Avoid claiming that a window-level 10% FPR is a packet-level 10% FPR.

## Adding other sources later

After acquiring and inspecting FRDR or EdgeFall files, add a source-specific loader that returns the unified record contract and an explicit unit/axis mapping. Print per-source counts and sample ranges, then test on **held-out subjects and held-out sources**. If body placement or gravity removal differs, report performance by source/placement and consider separate models or calibration. Do not silently mix gravity-removed wrist acceleration with gravity-included neck/waist acceleration and assume they share a distribution.
